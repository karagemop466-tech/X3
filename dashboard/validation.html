Validation Gates

The rules a strategy must survive before it is allowed to risk anything.

Twelve gates, all blocking. A strategy passes only when every gate is green on out-of-sample data. There are no graded results yet, so every strategy currently reads "no graded results".

Gate definitions

G-01Pre-registrationParameters, selection rule and staking rule are committed to the repo and timestamped before any graded result is recorded.Anything tuned after seeing results is hindsight, not skill.Blocking
G-02Minimum sampleAt least 200 graded bets at the declared unit size.Below roughly this sample the confidence interval on ROI is wider than any plausible edge.Blocking

G-04Break-even after vigModelled edge must exceed the book's overround on the relevant market.Beating a number that already contains the margin is the only definition of profitable.Blocking
G-05Statistical significanceTwo-sided test of hit rate against the implied break-even probability; nominal p below 0.05.Separates a real edge from a run of luck.Blocking
G-06Multiplicity controlBenjamini-Hochberg FDR at 10% applied within the declared strategy family.With many strategies, naive p-values manufacture winners.Blocking

Removing luck

  • Null model. Each bet is modelled as a Bernoulli trial at the book's implied break-even probability. The observed return is compared to the distribution of returns under that null.
  • Multiplicity. With 10 strategies, some will look good by chance. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 10% is applied inside declared families (G-06).
  • Stability, not just level. The 5th percentile of a bet-level bootstrap must be above zero (G-07), and the edge must survive the whole pre-declared parameter neighbourhood (G-08).
  • Concentration. An edge concentrated in one week or against one club fails G-10. That is usually luck wearing a costume.
  • Hindsight. Pre-registration (G-01) plus the holdout (G-03) mean a strategy can never be graded on data it was tuned on.
