Leaderboard: signal, staking and demotion

A competition that rewards the signal first and reports staking second, with a demotion gate that fires on arithmetic rather than on a vote.

Signal and staking are different questions

Signal asks whether the model ranked outcomes better than chance, and it involves no money. Staking asks what happened to a bankroll, which depends on unit size, price, the order of wins and losses, and luck. A leaderboard that ranks on the second while claiming the first is measuring luck. This page measures both, separately, and shows the arithmetic behind the split.

The demotion gate: any one trigger sends a live strategy back to paper

6 triggers, all computed
IDTriggerWhy this triggerThe arithmetic
D-130+ settled bets with negative cumulative flat-stake profitA 12-bet losing run is normal, so the trigger is on cumulative profit over the run rather than on consecutive losses. Thirty bets is short enough to act on and long enough that a real edge rarely sits under water for all of it.At a true 55% win rate, 30 losing bets in a row has probability 0.45^30 = 3.95e-11; at breakeven it is 2.15e-10. The trigger is therefore not a 50/50 call: it fires on a run that a genuine edge almost never produces.
D-2trailing 400-bet ROI below -3% while the pre-registered expectation was positiveA sharp registered edge almost never digs a 3%-below-turnover hole over 400 bets; a strategy with no edge digs it regularly. The trigger therefore separates a decayed edge from a genuine one without needing to know which it is in advance.P(ROI < -3% over 400 flat bets) = 0.0002 at a true 55% and 0.0061 at breakeven. Because the 55% figure is far below any conventional significance level, the trigger cannot be dismissed as an unlucky streak by a strategy that is merely breaking even.
D-3rolling 200-bet CLV negative for 3 consecutive re-ratingsCLV is the least noisy skill measure available and is not sensitive to variance in the outcome. Three consecutive negative windows is the standard process-quality failure signal.requires G-5 to be unblocked; cannot fire until prices exist.
D-4documented input defect: any strategy input found to be wrong, unverifiable, or from a source that was later retractedA correct result from wrong inputs is not a result. This is the trigger that applies to this repository's own history (a standings transcription had to be corrected once already).n/a - a defect is a defect regardless of sample size
D-5the strategy's own stated dependence term cannot be re-estimated from current dataFor combos and parlays, if the correlation that justified the joint price cannot be recomputed, the price is a guess and the strategy may not stay live.n/a
D-6reward payout would exceed the notional cap before G-3 is metStops a promotion being paid out of a sample too small to justify it.n/a
  • Demotion is automatic: any single trigger fires it. No human vote, no override on the same sample.
  • A demoted strategy returns to paper and may only be re-promoted on a NEW sample that post-dates the demotion, never on the sample that demoted it.
  • The demotion, its trigger, and the sample are published on the leaderboard page.
  • Losing a strategy is a normal cost of running many; hiding it is not permitted.
