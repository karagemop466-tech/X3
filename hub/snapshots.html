Price-snapshot store

Opening and closing numbers for every market we originate, with the honest statement that only one half of that pair exists.

What a snapshot store is for

A backtest needs the price that existed when a decision was made. Without a timestamped open and close, a result measures hindsight rather than a decision. The store therefore records our number with a timestamp, and it records the market only when a real market source exists - which today it does not.

Moneyline derivation, and the sign error that was caught

The moneyline is derived from the model spread through a normal win probability using a residual SD of 12.307 points measured on the 89 out-of-sample games. The sign convention in the canonical data file is that a positive home spread means the home club is receiving points. The first version of this module had that backwards, which would have priced every favourite at plus money. It was caught by checking each row against the spread and is documented here rather than quietly fixed.

Moneyline, priced fairly and checked

The model has no margin, so the two-way price must imply exactly 1.000 across the two sides. Each side is priced from its own probability (-100p/(1-p) and +100(1-p)/p), which is symmetric: a 0.612 home probability prices at -158 / +158. Deriving the away price by negating the home price instead would give -158 / +63, an implied sum of 1.226 - a 22% margin the model does not have. That defect existed in the first build of this module and is now a permanent gate.

Third-party prices: present, and deliberately unused

A separate tree in this repository (x3lab) carries Week-10 prices from a commercial odds page. They are surfaced here for transparency and are excluded from every calculation, because the project rule is that official claims use officially sourced numbers only. A single undated snapshot with no open/close pair could not support a closing-line-value calculation even if it were official.
