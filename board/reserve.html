The reserve list: 133 dated rows, and the trap inside them

The brief asks for the reserve list so that "not listed" never means "available". The repository held no reserve-list row at all. This board captures 133 dated entries from the official league feed, and then shows why the obvious way to use them is wrong.

1. Why "listed means unavailable" is wrong

The official feed is a transaction log, not a current-status roster. An entry records that a move happened on a date. It does not follow the player afterwards: it does not show him returning, being designated for return, being activated, or being released. So a March entry and a November entry look identical in the data.

7 players in the captured rows are listed Reserve/Injured and carry a designation that permits play (Questionable or Doubtful) in the Week-10 report. Reading "listed" as "out" would have silently removed 7 players who could still have played. The other 12 reserved players in the join are designated Out, which is consistent, and 2 carry no designation either way.

Two explanations fit and the captured evidence cannot separate them: (a) the player was activated off reserve before the report, which the log does not show; or (b) the report's designation is independent of reserve status. Either way the usable rule is not "is listed". A candidate rule — reserve date later than the report date implies unavailable — is published on Suggestions as a proposal, not applied, because the report page publishes no clock time, so "later than the report" cannot be evaluated yet.
