Injuries and depth: what is measured, and the one thing that is not

The brief wants the injury signal to be a surprise, never a level, and wants depth modelled from playing time rather than a jersey order. This page reports what the repository holds, checks it against the official report, and names the inputs that are still missing.

2. The signal is a level, and that is the bug

Why "Out" is not a surprise. An "Out" designation is already public, and the market has priced it. A signal built on the designation itself re-states information everyone holds. The brief's requirement is to measure the surprise — the gap between what was expected before the report and what the report then said. That requires two dated reads per game week so the expectation and the realisation can be differenced. The repository holds one read of Week 10. With one read there is no delta, and with no delta there is no surprise, and with no surprise the feature is a level. The requirement is therefore genuinely blocked, not skipped.

What would clear it: a dated capture series on the report's own publication days (Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, then the inactive list 90 minutes before kickoff), retained with hashes. The repository already has a scheduled workflow capable of this; what it has not yet accumulated is the history.

3. Depth of the replacement

The brief asks to track the depth of a replacement, not merely that a replacement happened, and forbids modelling depth from jersey order. The repository's replacement table holds 76 rows across the Week-10 designations. Their states:

The blank replacement cells are correct behaviour, not missing work: a club chart that does not list the injured player cannot say who replaces him. The honest output is a blank plus a reason.

The route that is verified, and the one that is not

  • Verified: official Game Books give actual starters and the inactive list for a game, with the league's own sheet as the source. That is a fact about a game that happened, not an inference about a depth chart.
  • Not verified: a league-wide snap-count source. Snap counts are the only defensible starter proxy if clubs publish nothing usable, and no official snap-count table is captured here, so the "model depth from playing time" requirement stays open.
  • Not verified: whether clubs update their depth pages after roster moves. Baltimore's quarterback depth after the Brissett trade is the live example; the chart could not be confirmed against the transaction on this pass.

4. Calibrating what "Questionable" means

Official text gives no percentage. The repository's past-season pilot joins designations to inactive lists and reports, with Wilson intervals: Questionable played 54 of 79 (68%, 95% CI 57–78); Out played 4 of 53 (7.5%, CI 3.0–17.9); Doubtful 1 of 10 (10%, CI 1.8–40.4). Those are descriptive rates on a pilot sample, not a feature, and the four "Out" players who played are published as flagged discrepancies rather than smoothed away. The n is too small to set a threshold, which is why the requirement is still open on Requirements.
