Leaderboard — signal and staking, scored apart

Signal decides the rank. Staking is reported, never rewarded.

Staking is scored separately from signal and can never promote a strategy. The design rule:

Ranking is decided by signal alone. Staking is printed beside it and can never move a strategy up — a staking run is a bet-size artefact and is exactly what a lucky run looks like. A row is ranked only when its signal metric has a 95% interval; rows without one are listed but marked unranked. The key is the LOWER bound of that interval on the gap to the no-skill value, so a wide interval ranks below a narrow one at the same point estimate.

Rewards

Rewards are notional. The leaderboard ranks strategies; it does not pay. Paying real money on a signal that has not cleared the sample-size, benchmark and uncertainty gates would fund luck, not skill. The reward structure is itself an open decision — see suggestion S-09 and question Q11.

How the competition is meant to work

  • One submission per strategy family per week, pre-registered before grading.
  • Ranked on signal, never on staking.
  • Losing bets are published with the winning ones — a leaderboard that hides its losers is a marketing page.
  • No re-fitting after grading. A strategy that changes its rule mid-season starts again at zero.
  • Rewards only once a strategy clears every blocking gate, and only on the signal score.
