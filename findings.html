What "a finding" means here

A finding is a statement computed from an official dataset, published with its source. An observation that the platform could not compute because a dataset is missing is listed on the Flagged for Review page instead of being stated loosely.

Methodological findings

  • The official injury report is a participation record, not a clearance. A club lists only the players it wants to report, so a player absent from the report cannot be assumed healthy. The platform records such players as not reported and never converts that into an availability claim.
  • Depth ordering is club-published and club-maintained. It is the only official route to a replacement's depth string, and its coverage is partial. The platform measures and publishes that coverage rather than filling the gaps.
  • Scoring a strategy against the model that priced the game measures agreement, not skill. This is the central methodological trap of an own-line platform, and it is why suggestion S-002 exists and why every strategy in the register uses a signal deliberately different from the line model's inputs.
  • A positive record is not evidence of skill at small sample. The platform therefore reports a skill share with a bootstrap interval and a permutation test against a pure-luck null, rather than a win rate alone.
  • Correlated parlay legs manufacture apparent edge. Multiplying correlated probabilities overstates the probability of the combination. The platform computes the correlation matrix and prices a parlay from the joint, not the product of the marginals.
  • Nothing is published that cannot be traced. Every table row links to the official page it came from, and the payload hash and fetch time are recorded for each source.
