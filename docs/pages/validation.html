Statistical foundations (verified at official hosts)

MethodWhat it doesVerified source
Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (PSR) Probability the true Sharpe exceeds a benchmark; robust to non-normal returns Bailey & López de Prado (2012), Journal of Risk 15(2):3–44 — listed in the SSRN PBO record
Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) PSR against the expected max Sharpe of N random trials — corrects selection bias. DSR ≥ 0.95 ⇒ ≥95% chance the edge is real after multiplicity Bailey & López de Prado (2014), J. Portfolio Management 40(5):94–107, DOI 10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094 · ssrn.com/abstract=2460551
Probability of Backtest Overfitting (PBO) via CSCV Probability that the "best" backtest is worse than median out-of-sample Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu (2015), J. Computational Finance · ssrn.com/abstract=2326253 · author PDF
False Strategy Theorem After ~1,000 skill-free trials the expected best Sharpe ≈ 3.26 — great backtests arise from noise Bailey & López de Prado (2014) · ssrn.com
MinTRL (minimum track record length) Minimum observations for a Sharpe estimate to clear a confidence threshold Bailey & López de Prado (2012), via ssrn.com/abstract=2326253

Parlay & prop-combo gates

  1. Every leg must independently pass G2 (positive CLV) before a combo can score.
  2. Combo EV from an explicit joint probability model — never naive multiplication (see Correlation).
  3. Correlation estimates need ≥ 3 seasons of play-by-play with bootstrap CIs; if the CI includes zero, treat the pair as independent and display the value blank.
  4. Max 4 legs per scoring entry (variance control). Cross-game parlays favored structurally: their margin math is transparent, unlike same-game correlation adjustments (industry analysis: source — secondary, flagged).
  5. Same-game parlays must beat the book's own correlation "tax," not merely feel correlated (source — secondary, flagged).
