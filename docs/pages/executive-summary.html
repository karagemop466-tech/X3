Key verified findings (2026-11-14)

  1. The market is very hard to beat at the close, but not at the open. Closing NFL prices are nearly calibrated; a 7,276-game replication found no classic angle profitable at closing prices (community study, source). Late-week line movement is driven mainly by sharp action, and it is predictable from early-week prices (Claremont thesis 2025, source). Implication: origination must beat the market early — signal classes S2/S3 (timing) are the primary skill channels.
  2. Books price to exploit public bias, not to balance action (Levitt 2004, source) — a structural reason carefully-built contrarian signals (S8) can persist.
  3. Backtests lie without gates. With ~1,000 skill-free trials the best "strategy" shows a Sharpe ≈ 3.26 by luck alone (Bailey & López de Prado 2014, source). Our gate stack (DSR ≥ 0.95, PBO ≤ 0.2, Monte-Carlo luck null, walk-forward OOS) makes "skill" a testable claim.
  4. Same-game correlation is real and already taxed. Books apply proprietary correlation adjustments that, per practitioner audits, exceed historical correlation by 5–15 points (source — industry secondary, flagged). Combo strategies must price joints themselves (our matrix values are honestly blank until computed).
  5. This week's sharpest verified deficiencies are at quarterback. Baltimore (Lamar Jackson on IR, Tyler Huntley out for season), Minnesota (Kyler Murray out), Seattle (Sam Darnold out), Miami (Malik Willis on IR → Kyle McCord's first start), Arizona (Carson Beck starting). Detroit is missing both starting safeties/CB1 and its center. Green Bay is missing Josh Jacobs and Micah Parsons. Details with links: Injuries.
