Validation gates and the demotion rule

A promotion gate decides who is allowed in. A demotion gate decides who is sent back out, and it matters more: a competition with no exit accumulates strategies that were once lucky. This page defines both, and it publishes a mechanical dry run that proves the demotion rule actually fires.

Self-checkfirst draft used a 40-bet window; the dry run showed the upper bound cannot fall below 0.5238 above 35% accuracy, so the window was widened to 100 bets
This gate was fixed by its own dry run. The first draft of D-1 used a 40-bet window. Run mechanically, that rule could not fire above 35% accuracy, because the Wilson interval at n=40 is too wide - it would have looked like protection while providing none. The window was widened to 100 bets and the upper-bound direction made explicit. The dry run is kept in the repository so the correction is auditable.

What is not simulated

D-2 to D-6 are defined but not dry-run: drawdown, CLV, duplicate-signal and data-failure gates all need a live strategy and a market price to exercise. They are published as definitions, not as tested controls, and the flagged page says so.
