Side, then total, then moneyline

The side is originated first, the total second, the moneyline third. That order is not cosmetic: it is what makes the three markets mutually consistent, and it is enforced by the code path in scripts/ops_model.py rather than by convention.

Why this order

  1. Side first. The side is the market with the most information per game: a rating that explains who wins covers the thing that actually settles most bets. It is fitted as a single ridge regression of the final margin on club identity plus a fitted home-field term, with neutral-site games carrying no home-field term.
  2. Total second. The total is a different quantity - pace, not dominance - and is fitted separately on the game total. Computing it after the side means the side cannot borrow information from a total that was fitted on the same game.
  3. Moneyline third, and only from the side. The moneyline is a probability, and probabilities are where books hide the vig. We generate it as a normal transform of the side - P(home) = Phi((net_home - net_away + hfa) / sigma) - so it can never disagree with the spread. If the side says home by 7 and the moneyline says home is a 40% underdog, the desk has a bug, and this ordering makes that bug impossible by construction.

Sigma is the residual standard deviation of the side model on the fitting window; it is the only parameter that turns a spread into a probability.

What the order costs us

Deriving the moneyline from the side means we inherit the side model's errors. Modern books fit totals and sides jointly with team-specific pace and pass/run splits; we do not. That is a known, published limitation (F-04), not a hidden one.
