Method, and why n is printed on every row

A correlation without its sample size is not a number, it is a rumour. r=0.87 at n=32 and r=0.87 at n=8 mean very different things, and the intervals on this page show it: the n=32 net-points coefficient (0.872) is decisive, while the n=8 team-mate coefficient (-0.143) spans almost the whole range.

The one that matters for parlays

A two-leg same-game parlay is only correctly priced with a joint probability. The one usable joint input here is the receptions-vs-yards relationship within a player, which is strongly positive (r=0.831, n=42) - but that is not the pair a parlay holds. For the pair a parlay actually holds (two teammates' yards), the measured coefficient is -0.143 at n=8, which is not distinguishable from zero. The honest conclusion: multiply the marginals, and print the caveat.

What is refused

  • No coefficient from a secondary source. If a number is not computed here from an official page linked on this page, it is not published.
  • No coefficient without n. Enforced by the build.
  • No causal language. These are co-movements in one partial season.
