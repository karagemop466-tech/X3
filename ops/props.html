Player props - fair lines and a fair test

Player props, originated from the official per-week log on each player's nfl.com page. The fair line is the mean of the player's prior weeks, rounded to the nearest half - deliberately the most boring possible estimator, because a more clever one on 5 players would be curve-fitting. A checksum in the build reconciles every logged week against the official season totals printed on the same page, so a transcription error cannot survive.

Read the over rate, not the MAE. Receiving-yards MAE is large in absolute terms because a receiver's week genuinely varies by 50+ yards; what matters is whether the over rate at our own line sits near 0.5. At n=27 the interval is wide enough to include it, so the honest statement is "not yet distinguishable from a fair line", not "fair".

Combos and parlays

Two-leg combos need a joint probability, and a joint probability needs a measured correlation. The desk measures one usable pair (CIN team-mates) and finds it indistinguishable from zero at n=8, which means the correct combo price is the product of the marginals and nothing better. That is why no prop parlay is published as a priced market: the evidence does not support a price yet.
