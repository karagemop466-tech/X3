Data policy

The price decision, the public/private boundary, and snapshot durability

Price-data policy PDP-1, decided 2026-11-15

No betting price is used, stored or implied anywhere in this desk. Every market column stays empty.

Why. No official NFL source publishes spreads, totals, moneylines or player-prop prices. Sportsbook prices are commercial data owned by the operator, not official league data, and using them requires a licence and a stored, timestamped capture. This repository holds neither.

What that forces.

  • fair vs market vs difference: the market and difference columns are empty; only the fair number is published.
  • ROI, closing-line value, drawdown and every staking figure are not computed and stay blank.
  • No strategy can be promoted to live, because promotion reads a staking-independent signal but demotion requires a live price to grade against.
  • Backtests are published as calibration and skill numbers, never as returns.

What would change it. A licensed, timestamped price feed captured pre-kickoff with a retained raw hash, plus a written terms review. Then, and only then, the market columns fill and the policy is superseded by PDP-2.

Alternatives considered and rejected.

  • Use a public odds aggregator — Rejected: not an official source, no licence, and the brief forbids inferred values.
  • Use the model's own line as the market — Rejected: that is arithmetic on our own number; it would fabricate a residual of zero.
  • Backfill prices later — Rejected: a price is only usable if captured before kickoff; a backfill is lookahead leakage.

Public analysis layer and private data layer

Requirement R-39 asks for a split before row-level feeds are ingested. The boundary used here: the public site publishes derived numbers — ratings, fair lines, hit rates, correlations — and the player-level rows stay in the repository's data files, which are the same files the league's own pages already publish. No licensed or private feed is redistributed, because none is held.

This is a documented boundary, not an enforced one. A private store is a decision for the reviewer (suggestion S-07), and the readiness table records the gate as open.

Snapshot storage and drift alarms

Requirement R-29, R-38, R-46 and R-62. Each capture carries a clock time and a SHA-256 over the page body; each snapshot carries a content hash over the derived numbers. Each parser pins an expected row count, and the gate fails the build when a count leaves its band rather than publishing a blank column.

What does not exist: an alert channel. The gate fails a CI run, which a human sees only if they look. Suggestion S-08 proposes opening an issue automatically.

X3 NFL desk · static site, no tracking, no client-side data collection. Every table row carries a link to the official page it came from, or an explicit UNVERIFIED badge. Benchmarks are labelled as benchmarks and in-sample numbers are labelled in-sample.
