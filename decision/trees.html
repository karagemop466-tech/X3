19 directories, each serving its own landing page

Counted from the filesystem, not from the notes: audit, council, crosscheck, dashboard, desk, docs, hub, lab, ledger, legacy, ops, origin, reliability, round2, signals, site, verification-hub, x3, x3lab. Four of them (hub, signals, origin, ops) each re-implement the same eight operating requirements on the same inputs, and council restates them as acceptance tests. The result is that a correction lands in one tree and the wrong number stays published in another - which is exactly what happened to the mislabelled correlation (L-07) and to the Munich venue string (G-04, F-D02), still on 3 rows of a served file.

This session adds a further tree and does not pretend that helps: it is the sixteenth, it implements no competing engine, and S-D2 exists to end the pattern. A seventeen-session project needs a merge policy, not another module.

Consolidation, as a decision rather than a cleanup

One canonical tree, one results feed, one price store, one correlation store, one leaderboard, one requirement register; the rest frozen read-only with a pointer from the landing page. That is S-D2. The mechanical half (choosing hub/ or signals/) is an afternoon; the judgement half is deciding which engine gets to be the model, because today the site answers that question three different ways in three different files.
